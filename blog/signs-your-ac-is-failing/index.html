Air Conditioner Warning Signs in Winston-Salem, NC

Home / Blog / Air Conditioner Warning Signs in Winston-Salem, NC

As the hottest days approach here in Winston-Salem, NC, our air conditioners become hard-working systems of our home comfort. They work tirelessly to keep our indoor spaces cool and comfortable. But like any mechanical system, air conditioners aren't designed to last forever and will eventually begin to show signs of wear and tear, indicating that a major repair or replacement might be on the horizon. Catching these indicators promptly can save you from a hot and inconvenient situation on the hottest day of the year, and potentially minimize damage in the long run. Here at Winston-Salem HVAC, we want to help you understand what to look out for.

Reduced Cooling Performance: Is Your Home Not as Cool as It Used to Be?

One of the most obvious and frustrating signs that your air conditioner is struggling is a reduction in its cooling capability. You could be setting the thermostat to colder temperatures only to still feel warm or humid indoors. Uneven cooling throughout your house is a tell-tale sign. This isn't just about comfort; it indicates a problem within the system. There are multiple potential causes for this problem. It could be a depleted refrigerant charge, which means your system isn't able to absorb and release heat effectively. Refrigerant is the lifeblood of your AC. Another common culprit is a dirty air filter, which restricts airflow and makes the unit work harder without achieving the desired cooling. Evaporator coils caked with grime can hinder performance, as they cannot efficiently transfer heat. The compressor itself, the engine of your AC, could be giving out. If you're experiencing inadequate cooling, it's time to have a professional from Winston-Salem HVAC diagnose the problem.

Strange Noises: When Your AC Sounds Off

A healthy AC unit typically produces a gentle, steady hum. If you start hearing unexpected auditory disturbances coming from your unit, it's a strong signal that something is amiss. A grinding or high-pitched squeal frequently suggests problems with worn motor bearings. These bearings, essential for the motor to turn freely, can wear down over time, leading to friction and eventual motor failure if not addressed. Clicking sounds, especially persistent ones, could indicate a failing relay or a problem with the electrical components. Loud banging or clunking is especially worrisome, as they might suggest a loose part hitting another component. Ignoring these noises can lead to more extensive damage. Addressing these auditory clues promptly can prevent a small issue from becoming a costly repair.

Increased Energy Bills: An Unexpected Surge in Utility Costs

One of the most insidious signs of a failing air conditioner is a creeping increase in your energy bills. If your bills are increasing without a change in thermostat settings, your AC unit might be working harder than it should to achieve the same level of cooling. Several issues can lead to this lack of efficiency. As mentioned, a clogged air filter reduces airflow, forcing the fan motor to work overtime. Leaky ducts can allow cooled air to escape into unconditioned spaces like your attic or crawl space, meaning your AC has to run longer to maintain the desired temperature. Units that are too old or not properly sized for your home are inherently less efficient. A lack of proper refrigerant makes the system strain. The compressor and fan motors can become less efficient as they age. When your energy bills are unexpectedly high, it's worth having Winston-Salem HVAC inspect your AC unit to identify the source of the inefficiency. We can help you understand if a repair or a more efficient replacement makes sense.

Poor Airflow: Is Air Barely Coming Out of the Vents?

Strong airflow is essential for both comfort and system efficiency. If you notice that the air coming out of your vents is weak, or if some rooms have significantly less airflow than others, it's a strong indication of an underlying problem. This problem can stem from various causes. The most frequent cause is a heavily soiled or obstructed air filter, which restricts the volume of air that can pass through your system. Obstructed return vents can impede the system's ability to pull air in. Beyond filters, issues with the fan motor itself can lead to reduced airflow. A struggling fan motor won't adequately circulate air through your home's ducts. Ductwork blockages or damage can severely limit airflow. Sometimes, even closed or partially closed dampers in your duct system can cause uneven airflow. Fixing poor airflow is vital not only for your comfort but also to prevent overworking your AC and reducing its longevity.

Excessive Moisture or Leaks: Puddles Around Your AC Unit?

A little condensation is fine, but if you see puddles or a lot of wetness, there's an issue. This is a clear red flag that something is wrong. The most common cause of water leaks is a clogged condensate drain line. Humidity removed from the air collects in a drain pan and exits via a condensate line during cooling. Algae, dirt, or debris can block this line, causing water to overflow the pan. This can result in damage to your property and even pose electrical risks. Leaks can also originate from a cracked or rusted drain pan. In more severe cases, a refrigerant leak can also cause ice to form on the evaporator coils, which then melts and overflows the drain pan. If you notice any unusual water accumulation around your AC unit, it's important to call for service right away to prevent further damage. Winston-Salem HVAC can diagnose the source of the leak and provide a lasting solution.

Frequent Cycling: When Your AC Can't Stay On

An AC unit should operate in steady cycles, cooling your home to the set temperature before turning off. If your AC starts turning on and off rapidly, a phenomenon called "short cycling," it's a serious indicator of a problem. In short cycling, the AC doesn't complete its cooling task, causing inefficiency and stress. Multiple factors can contribute to short cycling. An improperly sized unit (too large for your home) can sometimes short cycle because it cools the space too quickly and then shuts off before it can properly dehumidify. However, more often, short cycling points to a problem like a low refrigerant charge, a dirty air filter, or even a faulty thermostat. Electrical problems or a failing compressor can also cause short cycling. Whatever the reason, frequent short cycling accelerates wear on AC components, resulting in earlier breakdowns and higher energy use. An AC that short cycles isn't working properly and requires professional diagnosis.

Old Age: The Inevitable March of Time for Your AC

Even if your air conditioner isn't exhibiting any dramatic symptoms, its age is a crucial factor in predicting its eventual failure. Most central air conditioning systems are designed to last between 10 to 15 years, with some well-maintained units stretching to 20 years. An AC unit nearing or past its expected lifespan will be more prone to issues and less efficient. Many older ACs use R-22, an obsolete refrigerant that's expensive to service. Years of operation mean components in older systems are simply more fatigued. While maintenance prolongs life, there comes a time when repairs outweigh the cost of a new, efficient system. When your AC is over 10 years old and exhibiting other warning signs, it's time to think about replacement. A new, energy-efficient model can offer significant savings on your utility bills and provide superior comfort for years to come.
