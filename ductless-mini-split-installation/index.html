Mini splits exist for the rooms central air forgets. The garage workshop, the sunroom that turns into an oven by midafternoon, the addition the ducts never reached, the Winston-Salem house that was built without ductwork at all: each of these can be heated and cooled on its own by one or more wall-mounted heads. Our Approach to Ductless Mini Split Installation

Design is where mini split projects succeed or fail, and Winston-Salem HVAC does it deliberately. Each zone gets sized to its actual load, because a head too big for the room cools fast but leaves the air clammy. Multi-zone systems bring their own engineering: the outdoor unit's capacity has to be matched carefully across the connected heads, line set lengths and elevation changes must stay inside the manufacturer's tables, and head placement and style affects both comfort and how the room looks. Our experienced technicians walk every room with you, place heads where the physics and the sightlines agree, and route line sets cleanly, hidden where they can be and tidy where they cannot.

Our installation process is held to the same standard as our biggest central systems. Nitrogen goes into the line set first to prove it holds pressure, then a vacuum pump pulls it down to a level confirmed with a micron gauge. Flares are torqued and leak-tested one by one, since that joint is the classic failure point. Drainage is set up with real fall or a quiet condensate pump, and the refrigerant charge is adjusted to match the line length. Finally, every zone is commissioned in both modes and you get a clear explanation of how the remote actually works. Done Right

Why Professional Ductless Mini Split Installation Matters

Most failed mini splits we see in Winston-Salem come from installs that treated the job as simple. Refrigerant leaks at the flares are the headline problem: a joint that was poorly formed or never torqued to spec leaks slowly, and the homeowner finds out a season later when the head blows warm. Moisture is the next issue, because an install that skips the nitrogen pressure test and a micron-verified vacuum leaves water vapor inside a system whose inverter compressor is sensitive to it. Overlong or overly tall line set runs steal capacity quietly, and a drain line without enough fall will stain a wall as soon as humidity climbs.

Sizing discipline matters even more here than with central systems, because the smallest available heads are already plenty for many bedrooms; the wrong pick short-cycles, leaves humidity behind, and never settles. Our experienced technicians size each zone honestly, torque and leak-test every flare, pressure-test and evacuate to a measured vacuum, set charge adjustments to the line length, and commission each head in both modes. Refrigerant work is federally regulated, and the electrical side needs a properly sized circuit and disconnect. The result is what a mini split should be: silent, efficient, and uneventful for fifteen years, instead of a mysterious underperformer from a rushed weekend install.

Homeowner Tips

Maintenance Tips for Ductless Mini Split Installation

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Have the indoor blower wheel and coil professionally cleaned before they grow anything
  • Pull and rinse the indoor filter screens monthly, because they fill up much faster than you would expect
  • Let the inverter do its job: pick a temperature and leave it rather than toggling on and off
  • Glance at the condensate drain outlet in summer to confirm it is actually dripping
  • Brief defrost cycles and a little steam in winter are normal, but a unit encased in ice needs service
  • Book annual professional service to verify charge, flares, and drainage while fixes are cheap
  • Give the outdoor unit open space in every season, free of drifts, debris, and mowing clippings
